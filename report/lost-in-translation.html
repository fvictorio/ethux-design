Ethereum apps translate independently, each inventing its own vocabulary for the same actions. We read the translation files of seven of them, then measured what language does to wallet adoption on ethereum.org. Translation turns out to be a growth lever almost nobody is pulling, and the one place that rewards it takes every claim on faith.

Aave, in Russian

A word that moves money.

Open Aave in Russian and find the button that repays your loan. The label on it means reward. A person about to send money reads a promise of money arriving. Nobody at Aave chose that word. A pipeline chose it, and no Russian speaker was ever asked to stop it.

Aave · Russian · a lending app, where these words move your money
Repay→Вознаграждать→actually means "reward"
Collateral→Побочный→actually means "side / secondary"
Stake→Столб→actually means "pillar / post"
Withdraw→Отводить→actually means "divert away"
The same app's Spanish gets every one of these right. The Russian file reads like a dictionary picking the wrong sense of each word, and it shipped, because nobody was asked to look. This is the worst thing we found. Most of what we found is smaller and stranger.
The audit

Every app writes
its own dictionary.

In June we pulled the live translation files of six dapps plus Binance, in Portuguese, Spanish and Russian, and read them the way a native speaker would. Spanish has three words for "amount" and the apps use all of them. 1inch says "swap" three different ways to the same user in the same session. Uniswap ships a European Portuguese file written in Brazilian Portuguese. Each app translated alone, so the ecosystem reads like a city where every shop invented its own word for door.

"swap"→ Trocar Troca Fazer swap Efetuar swap Swap
✓ matches the prevailing word ✗ diverges, or contradicts itself all agree two forms three or more — not in that app

You can shrug at most of this. Synonyms are survivable, and a reader who meets "Monto" in one app and "Cantidad" in the next copes fine. Some of it locks people out: "slippage" left in English hides the one number that protects a trader from a bad price. And some of it ends where this report began, with a lending app promising a reward. The pattern held even where we expected discipline. Binance localizes professionally and still calls the same action "Convert" on its exchange and "Swap" in its own web3 wallet.

What 40,000 clicks say

Language decides
which wallets win.

The fair objection to everything above: does any of it move a real decision? ethereum.org's wallet chooser gave us a way to answer. Wallets available in the visitor's language are listed first, the rest are shuffled below, and the shuffle re-rolls often enough that we can tell "people click whatever is on top" apart from "people seek out their language." We counted about 40,000 outbound clicks across 36 language versions of the page. Both behaviors are real, and the split between them is the finding.

Language loyalty varies by market, and the variation is measurable. Thai, Indonesian, Vietnamese and Traditional Chinese readers click wallets in their language well beyond what list position predicts. Polish and Arabic readers behave differently: they scroll past the wallets that speak to them and pick global brands listed below, and Czech and Ukrainian clicks land almost exactly where position predicts. English proficiency is one plausible explanation, since the indifferent markets score high on it and the loyal markets lower, but our data measures clicks; the mechanism behind them remains a hypothesis.

For a wallet team the practical reading survives either way: localization earns measurable adoption in Bangkok, Jakarta and São Paulo, and comparatively little in Warsaw. Polish has three supporting wallets. Three.

Where the two halves meet

A checkbox nobody checks.

That ranking boost for language support comes from a field in a data file called languages_supported. Wallets declare it themselves. 92% of Russian readers' clicks follow it. And the first section of this report shows what an unverified language claim can contain: a repay button that says reward.

How a language claim becomes distribution
"languages_supported": ["ru", …]→listed first on the Russian page
listed first→position 1 alone draws 18% of clicks; the top five rows draw half
verified by→nobody
ethereum.org sits inside this finding too. The page's ordering puts Chinese wallets first for Chinese readers while its visible language menu defaults to English on every locale. The site steering well over a hundred thousand wallet clicks a year has the same coordination problem as the apps it lists. That is partly why we are writing this.
The proposal

One dictionary the whole
ecosystem can pull from.
Verified translation earns placement.

Every team already believes its own translations are fine, so a fix that runs on goodwill runs for about a month. This one runs on distribution. The glossary covers the roughly fifty terms that move money: send, repay, collateral, withdraw, seed phrase. Every locale file we audited was public, so verification is a script plus a native-speaker spot check. Tie ethereum.org's language boost to verified translations and a wallet that fixes its Russian gains placement, the same week, on a page sending twelve thousand high-intent clicks a month. Unverified claims still display. They stop earning rank.

01

AI drafts everything

Every term, every language, generated and published instantly. Translation was never the bottleneck.

02

Native speakers rule the hard calls

Contested choices go to the people who read them: translate "slippage" or keep it? Coin a word that does not exist yet? They decide and put their name on it.

03

Verification earns the boost

Apps pull the termbase; auditors and LLMs cite it via an open API; distribution surfaces reward the wallets that pass. One steward decision fixes the word everywhere at once.

An honest example of why humans stay in the loop. The current draft glossary translates "slippage" to deslizamiento with high confidence, yet the live apps keep the English "Slippage". Which is right is a native-speaker judgment. That call is the work.

What to do with this

Four readers, four moves.

Wallet teams

The cheapest growth you may have this quarter is a language. Demand in Polish, Indonesian and Thai is measured and public, and Polish has three competitors. One localization sprint, grounded in the termbase, buys a market.

Apps named here

The flagged terms are yours for the asking, and we will gladly re-audit. Aave's Russian needs four words fixed. Everything we read was public; your own native speakers can confirm it in an afternoon.

Translators

Roughly fifty safety-critical terms in your language need a ruling: translate, transliterate, or keep the English. The contested calls in the glossary are open for exactly this.

ethereum.org

One criterion added to the wallet listing policy, the language boost applies to verified translations only, turns this report into infrastructure. The audit script and the termbase are ready to serve it.
